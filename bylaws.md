# Bylaws of Columbia Gadget Works

*Ballot text v6, 19 October 2026. Proposed for ratification by the members at the annual meeting on Thursday 8 April 2027 (see `ballot.md`). Not yet in effect.*

These bylaws hold only what Missouri law requires, what protects members, and what should be hard to change. Everything else is policy (`policies/`), which the board can change at any time.

---

## 1. Name and purpose

1.1 Columbia Gadget Works ("CGW") is a Missouri public benefit corporation with members, under RSMo chapter 355. If these bylaws and the Articles of Incorporation differ, the Articles control.

1.2 CGW is organized exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, or scientific purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. It carries out that purpose by running a community workshop where people share tools, space, and skills.

1.3 As the Articles require:
- No part of CGW's net earnings benefits any member, director, officer, or other private person, except reasonable pay for services.
- No substantial part of CGW's activities is lobbying.
- CGW takes no part in any political campaign.
- CGW carries on no activity that a 501(c)(3) organization may not.

## 2. Members

2.1 **Classes.** CGW has three classes of member:
- **Full members** pay full dues, at any rate policy sets. Only full members vote. Full members aged 16 or 17 vote like any other full member.
- **Hardship members** have a dues waiver the board approved. They do not vote.
- **Legacy members** pay the legacy rate policy sets. They do not vote.

A person is a member while they meet the requirements set by policy for their class: a signed waiver and member agreement, and current dues or an approved dues waiver. Apart from voting and holding office, every member has the same rights and the same use of the Space, whatever their class.

2.2 **Good standing.** A member is in good standing from the day they join until their membership ends, unless they are suspended. Only full members in good standing may vote or hold office.

2.3 **Ending membership.** Membership ends when a member resigns, stops paying as policy describes, or is expelled. Membership gives no ownership of CGW property and cannot be transferred.

2.4 **Expulsion or suspension.** The board may expel or suspend a member only after all of the following:
- written notice to the member, with the reasons, at least 15 days beforehand;
- a chance for the member to be heard by the board, in person or in writing, at least 5 days before the expulsion or suspension takes effect;
- a vote of two-thirds of the directors eligible to vote on the matter, and at least three. A director involved in the matter does not vote.

2.5 **Access suspension.** When safety, property, or a person is at risk, any two directors may immediately suspend someone's access to the Space or to a tool. The suspension ends after 14 days unless the board confirms it. It does not affect the member's vote.

2.6 **Changes before a vote.** A policy change that affects who is a member or who may vote does not apply to a member vote whose notice has already been sent.

## 3. Board of directors

3.1 **Composition.** The board has five directors: the President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, and Membership Director. Members elect each seat separately.

3.2 **Terms.** Directors are elected at each annual meeting and serve until the next one, or until a successor is elected. Any full member in good standing who is 18 or older may run. No one holds more than one seat.

3.3 **Powers.** The board manages CGW. It decides everything not reserved to the members, including policy, the budget, contracts, appointments, membership, and discipline.

3.4 **Meetings.** The board meets at least quarterly, and announces each regular meeting to members at least 7 days ahead. Directors may attend by video or phone. The President or any two directors may call a special meeting on 48 hours' notice to the directors. Meetings are open to members, except closed sessions on discipline, personnel, hardship applications, or legal matters.

3.5 **Vacancies.** A seat becomes vacant when a director resigns, is removed, or stops being a full member in good standing. The board fills a vacancy by appointing an eligible full member until the next annual meeting. A director removed by the members may not be appointed again before the next annual meeting. If fewer than three directors remain, they may act only to fill vacancies or to call a member vote, and they must call a member vote to fill the empty seats.

3.6 **Removal.** Members may remove a director by member vote. The board may remove a director who, without being excused, misses two regular meetings in a row or half of the regular meetings in any twelve months.

3.7 **Unpaid.** Directors are not paid for board service. A director may be paid for teaching or other work on the same terms as any member.

3.8 **Conflicts of interest.** A director or appointed volunteer with a personal financial interest in a decision must disclose it and must not vote on it. The minutes record both. No one approves a payment to themselves. The board keeps a conflict-of-interest policy.

## 4. Officers

4.1 The **President** chairs meetings, sets agendas, and represents CGW.

4.2 The **Vice President** acts for the President when needed, and looks after the shop floor. Zone Bosses report to the Vice President, who oversees safety, facilities, and the space between zones.

4.3 The **Secretary** keeps the minutes and the official text of these bylaws and the policies. The Secretary sends notices and runs member votes.

4.4 The **Treasurer** keeps the books, prepares the budget and the annual financial report, and makes the state and IRS filings.

4.5 The **Membership Director** runs joining and orientation, keeps the member roster, follows up on missed dues, and brings hardship applications to the board.

4.6 **Signing.** The President or the Treasurer, or another director the board authorizes, signs contracts and other documents that bind CGW, within the spending limits set by policy.

4.7 The board may assign further duties by policy.

## 5. Voting

CGW has two kinds of vote:
- **Member votes** decide who runs CGW and what the bylaws say. They happen at the annual meeting, or when the board or members call one.
- **Board votes** decide everything else, including all policy. The board can hold one at any time.

Policy names the tools CGW uses for votes and notices: the governance repository that holds the official text, the member chat channel, and the member records system.

### Member votes

5.1 **What members decide.** Full members, voting under 5.5:
- elect and remove directors;
- amend these bylaws;
- approve dissolution, merger, or the sale of substantially all of CGW's assets;
- decide any question the board refers to them.

Only members can amend these bylaws.

5.2 **Annual meeting.** The annual meeting is held each April, on a date the board sets. Members elect the board there, and the Treasurer reports on the year's finances.

5.3 **Calling a vote.** The board may call a member vote at any time. If at least 5% of full members, and at least 3, ask for one in writing, the board must send notice of it within 30 days, and voting must close within 90 days of the request. If the board does not send notice in time, the members who asked may send it themselves.

5.4 **Notice.** Between 10 and 60 days before voting opens, the Secretary sends notice to every member: by email to members who have agreed to receive notices by email, at the address on their member record, and by mail to anyone else. The Secretary also posts it in the member chat channel. The notice states:
- each question, with the full text of any bylaw change;
- how and when to vote, including when voting closes;
- the quorum;
- the share of votes needed to pass.

5.5 **Who votes.** Any full member in good standing on the day voting opens may vote, with one vote each. Proxy voting is not allowed.

5.6 **Casting and recording votes.** Members vote by secret ballot, using the method set by policy. For each member vote, the board appoints one or two tellers from members who are not candidates. The Secretary and the tellers run the vote, record who voted and the totals, never how anyone voted, and certify the result. If the Secretary is a candidate, the tellers run the vote without the Secretary. A ballot stays open for at least 7 days. When there is a meeting, the ballot closes during it, at the time stated in the notice.

5.7 **Quorum.** A vote counts only if at least 10% of full members eligible to vote, and no fewer than 5, cast a ballot. Abstentions count toward quorum but are not votes cast. If a vote fails for lack of quorum, the directors stay in office and the board holds the vote again within 60 days.

5.8 **Passing.** A question passes by a majority of votes cast, except that amending these bylaws, dissolution, merger, or the sale of substantially all assets needs two-thirds of votes cast.

5.9 **Elections.** Nominations open with the notice and close as policy sets.
- A candidate runs for only one seat.
- The candidate with the most votes for a seat wins, and a tie goes to a runoff ballot.
- A seat with one candidate is on the ballot as a yes-or-no question, and the candidate is elected by a majority of votes cast.
- A seat with no candidate, or whose only candidate is not elected, is a vacancy filled under 3.5.
- There are no write-in votes.

5.10 **Corrections.** The Secretary may correct typos, formatting, and cross-references in these bylaws without a vote if the meaning does not change, and lists each correction in the next member-vote notice.

5.11 **Adoption.** These are CGW's first bylaws. The board adopts them subject to ratification by the members, and they take effect when ratified under 5.7 and 5.8. For the ratification vote, the voters are the people who are full members under section 2 on the day voting opens. The board election held with the ratification vote is conducted under these bylaws, and it takes effect only if they are ratified. If they are not ratified, the directors stay in office and put revised bylaws to the members within 120 days. The date and result of each ratifying or amending vote are recorded in the governance repository.

### Board votes

5.12 **Policy.** The board adopts, changes, and repeals policy by a vote of at least three directors, at any time. A policy takes effect when it is entered in the official text in the governance repository, and it may not conflict with these bylaws or the Articles.

5.13 **Voting in writing.** Directors may vote on a policy change in writing, through the governance repository, as policy describes. A director with a conflict under 3.8 does not vote. The change passes when three directors approve it, and it is then entered and takes effect. The repository's record is the record of the vote. If every director in office approved, the change is final. Otherwise the board confirms it at its next meeting, and if the board does not confirm it, the Secretary removes it from the official text the same day.

5.14 **Voting at meetings.** Any other board decision is made at a board meeting. Three directors are a quorum, and a motion passes by a majority of directors present; a tie fails. A policy change still needs three directors voting for it. The Secretary records each vote in the minutes.

## 6. Delegation

6.1 The board may create zones and other roles, and delegate authority to the volunteers it appoints to them. Each delegation is set out in policy, and the board may withdraw it.

6.2 Appointed volunteers are not officers or directors. They may not sign contracts or commit CGW to ongoing costs unless the board authorizes them in writing.

6.3 A committee that exercises any of the board's authority has at least two directors on it.

## 7. Indemnification

7.1 CGW indemnifies its directors, officers, and appointed volunteers for actions taken in good faith on CGW's behalf, to the fullest extent Missouri law allows.

7.2 CGW may pay a person's reasonable defense costs as they arise, if the person agrees in writing to repay them should it turn out they are not entitled to indemnification.

7.3 The board may buy insurance for CGW's directors, officers, and appointed volunteers.

## 8. Dissolution

8.1 If CGW dissolves, the board first pays, or makes provision for, all of CGW's liabilities. It then distributes the remaining assets exclusively for CGW's purposes, to one or more organizations qualifying under Section 501(c)(3). Any assets not distributed this way are disposed of by the Circuit Court of the county where CGW's principal office is located, for those same purposes. This follows the Articles.
